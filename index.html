Distribution equity. The owned channels are real and active. Storytelling equity. This is the part worth evolving. The audit is on what changes when the same craft and audience-first thinking gets applied to the rest of the catalog.

That gap is what this audit is really about. The hard part, building the firm, the team, the client outcomes, and a real publishing rhythm, is already done. The work below is on what flows through the system you've already built.

03 Lead With Value

The Legendeering Framework

The methodology behind every Talex production. Five directives, one core principle.

Legendeering is Talex's proprietary strategy. The core principle is straightforward: lead with value, always. The brand earns trust by giving without expectation, and that trust grows over time because people keep choosing to watch.

Five directives drive it:

  • ·Give Without Expectation. Value delivered without strings. The kind of generosity that builds loyalty money cannot buy.
  • ·Be Relentlessly Authentic. Say true things. Show real work. Speak like a human being.
  • ·It's Not About You. It's About Them. The audience has to come first. It's all about their wants and needs and making something they will choose to watch.
  • ·Start With Story, Follow With Facts. Story gives information shape, context, and emotional weight. Facts alone rarely move people.
  • ·Consistency Is Key. Trust is built in moments, repeated over time.

Each idea below is one of these directives in production. "The Exit" starts with story before any coaching framework. "Coach As Operator" gives without expectation by letting the audience meet the person behind the bio card. The video adaptation of Mark's collapse essays puts the audience first by treating the question of how good companies fall apart as the actual subject of the work.

CCI has built genuine operator and distribution equity. The three ideas below are what that equity looks like when it consistently lands as story instead of stat block.

04 Three Ideas For Video

Formats That Would Compound What You've Built

Each idea passes the four tests: audience-first, entertaining as a stand-alone watch, novel rather than a remix, and built to compound across episodes. None of them require a new audience. They just need a home.

01
"The Exit"
A returning episodic documentary series. Each episode is one CCI-coached CEO, filmed across the run-up to a real exit. Sarah Dusek and Under Canvas. Richard Paek and Jiffy Lube. Rich Balot and Victra. One founder, one company, one inflection point, and the actual outcome on the other side. Documentary craft, the founder on camera, the coach in the supporting role, the brand behind the story. The names on the homepage are the casting pipeline. Specific subjects come out of pre-production with the coach and the founder. The format is what matters: each film stands alone, the series builds into a body of work the firm can point to for the next ten years.
02
"Coach As Operator"
A portrait series profiling individual Partners and Certified Coaches in the context of the company they used to run, before the camera pulls back to how they coach today. Your portrait would open inside Kantar Consulting (twelve people scaled to over a thousand in five years) or inside the ChangeMakers merger of six agencies, then connect those operating decisions to the work you do with a current CEO. The format works for any coach on the sixty-plus roster who carries a real operating story behind them. It answers the question every founder has before they hire a coach: who am I actually going to be working with, and what did they actually do.
03
"The Collapse" · Mark Moses's Essay Series, On Film
Mark is shipping a 25-part written series on famous business collapses. Theranos. Bear Stearns. Twenty-three more on the way. Each part already lands on a transferable lesson for current CEOs. The film treatment takes the same arc into video: archival footage of each company at the peak, named characters at the decision points that produced the fall, and Mark's framing of what the audience can use today. The essays are the storyboard. The series builds because every CEO in CCI's audience wants to know how the famous collapses actually happened, and almost none of them has the time to read 25 articles.
